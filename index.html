Chicago Crime Data Analysis

This dashboard presents a comprehensive analysis of crime data from the City of Chicago, utilizing the official Chicago Data Portal's publicly available crime dataset. The data encompasses over 8 million crime incidents reported to the Chicago Police Department.

About This Project

Data Source: Chicago Data Portal (data.cityofchicago.org)

Methodology: This analysis processes crime incident data using R, focusing on temporal patterns, crime type distributions, and long-term trends. The data is automatically updated monthly to reflect the latest reported incidents.

Processing Pipeline:

  • Download crime data via Socrata Open Data API
  • Extract relevant fields (date, crime type, arrest status, domestic violence flag)
  • Aggregate data into monthly, daily, and hourly summaries
  • Generate visualization-ready JSON files for web display
  • Automated monthly updates via GitHub Actions

Data Coverage

The dataset includes all reported crimes from 2001 to present, updated monthly. This visualization shows the total number of crimes recorded each year, revealing long-term trends in Chicago's crime landscape.

Crime Trends Over Time

Analyzing monthly and yearly crime trends reveals patterns in Chicago's public safety landscape. These visualizations help identify long-term improvements or areas of concern.

Monthly Crime Trends

This chart displays the total number of crimes reported each month over the past several years. Monthly data reveals seasonal patterns and recent trends more clearly than yearly aggregates. Notice any cyclical patterns or significant changes in crime rates over time.

5-Year Trend Analysis

Focusing on the most recent 5 years provides insight into current trends and recent policy impacts. This view helps answer: Is crime increasing or decreasing in recent years? Are there any sudden changes that correspond to major events or policy changes?

Crime Type Analysis

Not all crimes are equal. This section breaks down which types of crimes are most common and how different crime categories have evolved over time.

Top Crime Types

This chart shows the most frequently reported crime types in Chicago. Property crimes like theft typically dominate the statistics, but violent crimes, though less frequent, are particularly concerning for public safety. Understanding this distribution helps allocate law enforcement resources effectively.

Crime Types Over Time

This stacked area chart reveals how different crime types have trended over time. Some crimes may be decreasing while others increase. For example, motor vehicle theft patterns often differ significantly from assault patterns. This visualization helps identify which specific crime categories require focused intervention.

Temporal Crime Patterns

When do crimes occur? Understanding daily and weekly patterns can inform police patrol schedules and help residents make informed safety decisions.

Crimes by Hour of Day

This chart shows crime frequency across 24 hours. Notice how crime patterns vary throughout the day - typically peaking in late afternoon/evening hours and dropping during early morning. This information is valuable for optimizing police patrol schedules and understanding when residents should be most vigilant.

Crimes by Day of Week

Are weekends more dangerous than weekdays? This visualization breaks down crime frequency by day of the week. Typically, weekends see different crime patterns than weekdays, with certain crime types (like assault) peaking on Friday and Saturday nights.

Year-Over-Year Comparison

Comparing the current year to previous years helps evaluate whether recent trends represent improvement or deterioration in public safety.

Current Year vs Previous Years

This chart compares monthly crime totals for recent years, making it easy to see whether the current year is tracking higher or lower than previous years. Month-by-month comparison accounts for seasonal variations and provides a clear picture of year-over-year change. A downward trend indicates improving public safety, while an upward trend suggests increasing challenges.

Annual Totals

This simple bar chart shows total crimes per year, providing a clear view of long-term trends. Significant changes between years often correspond to major policy changes, economic conditions, or data collection methodology updates.

Advanced Analytics

Deep-dive analysis into arrest rates, crime concentration, seasonal patterns, and detailed temporal heatmaps to uncover hidden insights in Chicago's crime data.

Arrest Rate Trends

This chart shows the percentage of reported crimes that resulted in arrests over time. Declining arrest rates may indicate challenges in law enforcement resources or changes in crime reporting practices. Rising rates suggest improved clearance rates and investigative effectiveness.

Crime Concentration Analysis

This pie chart reveals what percentage of total crimes are concentrated in the top 5 crime types versus all others. High concentration indicates a few crime types dominate, while low concentration suggests more diverse criminal activity. This helps prioritize law enforcement strategies.

Domestic vs Non-Domestic Crimes

Comparing domestic-related crimes (family/household violence) to non-domestic incidents reveals important social patterns. Domestic crimes often require different intervention strategies, including social services and victim support programs, compared to other crime types.

Year-Over-Year % Change

This bar chart shows the percentage change in total crimes from one year to the next. Positive values (red bars) indicate crime increased compared to the previous year, while negative values (green bars) show improvement. This helps identify whether recent trends are accelerating or reversing.

Hour × Day of Week Heatmap

This table shows crime frequency across every hour of every day of the week. Darker blue colors indicate higher crime activity. Notice patterns like late-night weekend spikes or weekday afternoon increases. This data is crucial for optimizing patrol schedules and resource allocation.

3-Year Monthly Comparison

Compare the most recent three years month-by-month to see if seasonal patterns are consistent and whether the current year is tracking better or worse than previous years. This helps separate seasonal variation from actual trends.
